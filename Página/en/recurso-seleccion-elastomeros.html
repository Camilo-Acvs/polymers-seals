How to Choose the Right Elastomer for Oil & Gas Seals

Material selection is an engineering decision, not a catalog preference. Real field conditions combine temperature gradients, differential pressures exceeding 15,000 PSI in HPHT applications, exposure to sour gas (H₂S and CO₂), and dynamic movement — and no single elastomer performs optimally across all of these variables at once. This guide compares the 7 elastomeric compounds most used in Oil & Gas sealing.

NBR — Nitrile Butadiene Rubber

The most widely used elastomer in Oil & Gas. A synthetic copolymer of acrylonitrile and butadiene with exceptional resistance to mineral oils, hydrocarbons and fuels. Acrylonitrile content (18%–50%) determines the balance between chemical resistance and low-temperature flexibility.

  • Temperature range: -40 °C to +120 °C
  • Hardness: 40 – 90 Shore A
  • Tensile strength: 10 – 25 MPa
  • Elongation at break: 100 – 600%
  • Compatibility: recommended for mineral oils — not recommended with H₂S, glycols, aromatic solvents or steam

HNBR — Hydrogenated Nitrile Butadiene Rubber

Obtained by selective hydrogenation of NBR, removing unsaturated butadiene groups. Considerably improves thermal, ozone and aging resistance while maintaining excellent compatibility with oils and hydrocarbons. It is the reference material for critical applications where conventional NBR falls short.

  • Temperature range: -40 °C to +150 °C (peaks up to +160 °C)
  • Hardness: 50 – 90 Shore A
  • Tensile strength: 20 – 31 MPa
  • Abrasion resistance: very high
  • Compatibility: recommended for mineral oils and H₂S (sour gas) — not recommended with glycols or aromatic solvents; limited in steam

XNBR — Carboxylated Nitrile Butadiene Rubber

A terpolymer of butadiene, acrylonitrile and acrylic acid. Carboxyl groups enable additional cross-linking that significantly increases mechanical strength and abrasion resistance over conventional NBR, while maintaining similar chemical compatibility.

  • Temperature range: -30 °C to +120 °C
  • Hardness: 50 – 90 Shore A
  • Tensile strength: 15 – 30 MPa
  • Abrasion resistance: superior to NBR
  • Compatibility: recommended for mineral oils — not recommended with H₂S, glycols, aromatic solvents or steam

FKM — Fluorocarbon Rubber (Viton®)

A high-performance fluorinated elastomer with exceptional chemical and thermal resistance thanks to its high fluorine content. Practically inert to most aggressive fluids found in Oil & Gas — the preferred material for HPHT applications and severe sour-gas environments.

  • Temperature range: -20 °C to +200 °C (peaks up to +230 °C)
  • Hardness: 60 – 90 Shore A
  • Tensile strength: 10 – 20 MPa
  • Compression set: very good
  • Compatibility: recommended for mineral oils, H₂S and aromatic solvents — not recommended with glycols or steam

Neoprene — Polychloroprene (CR)

The first commercially developed synthetic rubber. Chlorine in its polymer chain gives it good resistance to oxidation, ozone and weathering, with an adequate balance between mechanical properties and chemical resistance for medium-duty surface applications.

  • Temperature range: -40 °C to +120 °C
  • Hardness: 40 – 80 Shore A
  • Abrasion resistance: moderate
  • Compatibility: limited in mineral oils and glycols — not recommended with H₂S, aromatic solvents or steam

Polyurethane (PU)

Distinguished by superior mechanical properties: the highest abrasion and extrusion resistance among materials used in Oil & Gas, with tensile strength two to three times that of NBR. Two main types exist — Polyester (AU) and Polyether (EU) — depending on the service environment.

  • Temperature range: -40 °C to +100 °C
  • Hardness: 60 – 98 Shore A
  • Tensile strength: 25 – 60 MPa
  • Abrasion resistance: excellent
  • Compatibility: recommended for mineral oils, limited in H₂S — not recommended with glycols, aromatic solvents or steam

EPDM — Ethylene Propylene Diene Monomer

A non-polar base elastomer (ethylene, propylene and a tertiary diene monomer) with excellent resistance to ozone, UV radiation, aging and polar fluids such as water, steam and glycols. It is the material of choice for water and steam systems — not for hydrocarbon service.

  • Temperature range: -45 °C to +150 °C (peaks up to +175 °C)
  • Hardness: 40 – 90 Shore A
  • Compression set: very good
  • Compatibility: recommended for glycols and steam — not recommended with mineral oils, H₂S or aromatic solvents

Selection criteria

No elastomer delivers optimal performance across all variables at once: materials with higher chemical resistance tend to have lower elongation and higher stiffness; those with higher abrasion resistance may show greater compression set at high temperature; those with wider thermal range often have lower compatibility with polar fluids. Correct selection always starts from real service conditions — temperature, differential pressure, contact fluid and type of movement — not from a catalog preference.

For conditions more severe than a conventional elastomer
