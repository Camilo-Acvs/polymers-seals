MATERIALS SCIENCE

Elastomeric Materials & Compounds

The right material is the first engineering decision in any seal. This is the family of elastomers we formulate and machine for Oil & Gas operating conditions.

MATERIAL SELECTION

There is no universal elastomer. Every compound is a balance between chemical resistance, temperature range, mechanical behavior and cost. Selecting the right material for the fluid, pressure and type of movement of each application is what separates a seal that lasts from one that fails in service.

Each material's nomenclature follows the international standards ISO 1629 and ASTM D1418.[3] The temperature ranges shown are typical for standard compounds and can be extended through specific formulation.

NBR
Nitrile (Buna-N)
ISO 1629 / ASTM D1418: NBR
−35 °C to +120 °C

The industry's workhorse. Nitrile offers the best performance-to-cost ratio when the contact fluid is a hydrocarbon.

Strengths
  • Excellent resistance to mineral oils, fuels and hydraulic fluids[2]
  • Good mechanical strength and abrasion resistance
  • Limitation: low resistance to ozone, weathering and high temperature
In our portfolio
  • Inner & outer seals, stuffing box, swab cups
  • General-service packing and gaskets
HNBR
Hydrogenated nitrile
ISO 1629 / ASTM D1418: HNBR
−35 °C to +150 °C

The evolution of nitrile for severe conditions. It keeps NBR's oil resistance but raises the thermal ceiling and sour-gas resistance.

Strengths
  • NBR oil resistance with continuous service up to +150 °C, peaks near +170 °C[1]
  • Excellent resistance to ozone and sour gas (H₂S)
  • Higher tensile and abrasion resistance than NBR
In our portfolio
  • HPHT stuffing box, BOP seals, packer elements
  • HNBR vee packing as reference material
XNBR
Carboxylated nitrile
ISO 1629 / ASTM D1418: XNBR
−30 °C to +150 °C

Nitrile modified with carboxyl groups to maximize wear resistance, ideal where abrasion is the dominant failure mode.

Strengths
  • Abrasion and tear resistance far superior to standard NBR[5]
  • High tensile strength to withstand pressure and mechanical stress
  • Retains the oil resistance of the nitrile family
In our portfolio
  • High-wear dynamic seals and rod wipers
  • Cups and elements under continuous friction
FKM
Fluoroelastomer (Viton®)
ISO 1629: class M · ASTM D1418: FKM
−20 °C to +200 °C

The standard for high temperature and aggressive fluids. When NBR and HNBR reach their chemical or thermal limit, FKM takes over.

Strengths
  • Outstanding resistance to heat, mineral oils, fuels and ozone[2]
  • Compatible with a broad range of chemicals and solvents
  • Limitation: poor low-temperature behavior
In our portfolio
  • Seals for high temperature and aggressive fluids
  • Valve seals, hammer union seals, diaphragms
CR
Neoprene (polychloroprene)
ISO 1629 / ASTM D1418: CR
−40 °C to +120 °C

A well-balanced general-purpose elastomer, valued for weather and flame resistance rather than extreme chemical resistance.

Strengths
  • Good resistance to weathering, ozone and flame
  • Moderate resistance to oils and abrasion
  • Good mechanical behavior over a wide thermal range
In our portfolio
  • Pipe gaskets and joints
  • Weather-exposed components
PU
Polyurethane
ISO 1629: class U · ASTM D1418: AU / EU
−30 °C to +80 °C

Unbeatable in abrasion and load capacity. When mechanical wear and extrusion pressure are the challenge, polyurethane is the answer.

Strengths
  • Exceptional abrasion and tear resistance
  • High load capacity and extrusion resistance
  • Limitation: sensitive to hydrolysis and sustained heat
In our portfolio
  • Pack off, pipeline pigs, swab cups
  • Wipers and high-wear elements
EPDM
Ethylene-propylene-diene
ISO 1629: class M · ASTM D1418: EPDM
−45 °C to +150 °C

The specialist for water, steam and polar fluids. With one critical caveat: it is not compatible with petroleum hydrocarbons.

Strengths
  • Excellent with hot water, steam and polar fluids[2]
  • Very good resistance to ozone, weathering and aging
  • Important: do NOT use with petroleum oils or fuels
In our portfolio
  • Water and steam service
  • Drill pipe wipers and victaulic gaskets (water)
POLYKRAN®
Aramid-reinforced compounds
Proprietary technology · NBR · HNBR · FKM bases
per base

Our proprietary technology: aramid pulp integrated into high-performance rubber matrices. The fiber adds a tensile strength near 3,000 MPa and thermal stability that does not melt but decomposes above 450 °C.[1][4]

Strengths
  • Maximum resistance to extrusion and permanent deformation
  • Superior performance in HPHT and sour-gas conditions
  • Three variants: NEKLON® (NBR), H-NEKLON® (HNBR), VIKLON® (FKM)
In our portfolio
  • Aramid fiber vee packing
  • Seals for the most demanding conditions

SELECTION CRITERIA

How we choose the right material

Three variables define the compound before manufacturing any seal.

Contact fluid

Defines chemical compatibility. Hydrocarbons → nitrile family or FKM. Water and steam → EPDM. It is the first variable that rules materials out.

Temperature and pressure

They bound the service range. Sustained high temperature or HPHT pushes toward HNBR, FKM or POLYKRAN®; extreme cold rules out FKM.

Type of movement

Static, dynamic or reciprocating. Continuous movement and abrasion favor polyurethane, XNBR or aramid reinforcements.
